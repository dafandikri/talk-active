The general problem

A polished rehearsal can still leave important evidence implicit.

A student can know the scoring rubric and still reach Q&A without knowing which claim is unsupported. Our working hypothesis: rubric-grounded evidence review can help students find and repair that gap before the real evaluation.

Talk-Active in one sentence

It helps a student find and rehearse the claim their evaluator has the strongest reason to challenge.

The general problem

A polished rehearsal can still leave the important evidence implicit.

A student can know the scoring rubric and still reach Q&A without knowing which important claim is unsupported.

Our working hypothesis: rubric-grounded evidence review can help students find and repair that gap before the real evaluation. This is the behaviour the current prototype is built to test.

Who it is for

Students preparing for a real, rubric-driven evaluation.

The initial focus is an Indonesian university student preparing a competition pitch with a published rubric and limited access to a rehearsal partner. Thesis defenses and scholarship interviews are adjacent contexts, not separate products.

BeachheadCompetition pitch

A real scoring matrix, a short presentation, and judge Q&A.

AdjacentThesis defense

A prepared argument that must survive examiner questions.

AdjacentScholarship interview

Claims and examples that need to remain defensible under follow-up.

The product loop

One event stays connected from rubric to progress.

Each step produces the context for the next one, so preparation becomes a repeatable workspace instead of an isolated score.

  1. 1
    Project

    Keep the event, deadline, draft, rubric, and history together.

  2. 2
    Rubric

    Enter the evaluator's actual criteria and expected evidence cues.

  3. 3
    Attempt

    Paste a pitch or transcript and complete one natural rehearsal.

  4. 4
    Cited evidence

    See the supporting transcript span or the cues that stayed missing.

  5. 5
    Hardest question

    Defend the weakest rubric criterion under one grounded follow-up.

  6. 6
    Saved progress

    Return to recurring gaps and revise the next attempt deliberately.

What makes it different

The rubric is the starting point, and evidence is visible.

Feedback is traceable to the criterion the evaluator wrote and to what the student actually said. A verdict must show an exact transcript span or an explicit list of missing cues.

What exists today

A working recurring loop, with explicit boundaries.

This brief describes the current prototype. It does not add a capability that the workspace cannot demonstrate.

Working in the prototype

  • Persistent projects, drafts, rubrics, and session history on this device
  • Pasted-transcript practice with optional browser dictation
  • Per-criterion evidence review and a grounded judge question
  • Saved defense results and progress across attempts

Boundaries we state plainly

  • Current analysis uses deterministic cue matching, not semantic understanding.
  • Evidence coverage is formative transcript context, not confidence or speaking ability.
  • Device-local persistence is not account-based or cross-device storage.
  • Raw audio is not stored; dictation availability depends on the browser.

What judges should watch

The differentiator is visible in the review—not in a feature list.

  1. Open

    Show the saved Finals project and its active rubric.

  2. Attempt

    Paste a short pitch excerpt and review the result.

  3. Trace

    Point to the exact quote or missing cues behind one criterion verdict.

  4. Defend

    Answer the question tied to the weakest criterion.

  5. Return

    Save, reload, and show that the progress remains.

The shared team language

Find the weak claim before the judge does.

Success is not a speaking score. It is that a student changes a specific claim after the evidence review, then returns for another attempt.
